# `hardn confirm`'s clean-up, and best-effort releases (`pkg/engine/confirm`, P-40, P-41)

> **Unit:** refactor plan Phase 3, `next/p3-confirm-cleanup` (plan §2, row `p3-confirm-cleanup`, after `next/p3-output`; brief 2.3 step 5, "Confirm the change from a new connection, then remove the old port rule"; brief 2.4; brief §2, the decisions "Confirm-or-revert" and "Support what each vendor still supports"). Decisions it implements, both the maintainer's of 2026-10-19, recorded on `next/v0.4` (bda87af) and not in this stack: **[P-40](../refactor/proposals.md#p-40)**, "`hardn confirm` does it": once a port change is confirmed from a new connection to the new port, the same command plans and applies the clean-up (the held port dropped from `00-hardn.conf`, the reload, the ufw rule hardn added for it), and rolls it back in-process if a step fails; and **[P-41](../refactor/proposals.md#p-41)**, best-effort releases are "changed, with a warning in the plan" that the release is best effort and untested. It closes the README's `p3-engine` item "After the confirmation" ([docs/safety/README.md](../safety/README.md)), which [engine.md](engine.md) §7 and [steps.md](steps.md) §2.9 left to the maintainer. No V-number: the held port is confirm-or-revert's own leftover ([revert.md](../safety/revert.md) §7).
> **Status:** built and tested on macOS against the recording fake (`hostfake`): the claim and the clean-up on `internal/tools/p2drive`'s fake VM, after the 2230 change made by p2drive (as the VM runs make it) or by the engine, on Debian's `ssh.service`, Ubuntu's `ssh.socket` and Alpine's OpenRC; `cmd/hardn`'s wiring through its seams, with no binary run. Nothing has run on Linux: everything host-specific is "written, not run (gate 5)" (§4).

A port change holds the old port while it waits for confirmation: `00-hardn.conf` has `Port 22` beside `Port 2230`, and ufw lets both through ([reload.md](../safety/reload.md) §2.5, [steps.md](steps.md) §2.2). Until this unit, `hardn confirm` claimed the journal, stopped the revert timer and stopped there, so port 22 stayed open after every confirmed port change. Now, once the claim succeeds, `hardn confirm` cleans up from the connection that confirmed: it drops the held port, reloads sshd and deletes the ufw rule the change added for it, through the engine, under the run lock, journalled write-ahead, and rolled back in this process when a step fails. The confirmation stands whatever the clean-up does, and the command says what was cleaned up, what was not, and how to finish it, with `hardn confirm` again or by hand. Separately, every plan made on a best-effort release (Ubuntu 22.04, Alpine 3.22 and 3.21) now carries a warning finding, `PLATFORM_BEST_EFFORT`.

## 1. Why

- **P-40.** `steps.PlanCleanup` and `CleanupOf` were built by `next/p3-steps` ([steps.md](steps.md) §2.9), but nothing planned them: the old port and hardn's ufw rule for it stayed open after the confirmation until something removed them. That locks no one out, but it leaves the host less hardened than the change claimed. P-40 chose `hardn confirm` over the next run (which loses the held port once another lockout-risk change is confirmed, since `confirmed.json` keeps one change) and over the applying session (which may be gone).
- **P-41.** The engine's support gate changed best-effort releases and refused the rest; whether to change them was `next/p3-engine`'s reading (engine.md §7). The maintainer kept it, with a warning, so that the user knows hardn is not tested on the release.

## 2. Guarantees

### The claim and what the change held (`Claim`)

1. **The claim is revert's, unchanged** (`revert.Confirm`: `CheckConfirm`, the rename of `pending.json` to `confirmed.json`, the timer stopped; [revert.md](../safety/revert.md) §2.5, §2.11, §2.12). Then `steps.CleanupOf` of the journal just claimed says what the change held: `Drop`, the ports its reload restores that no `ConfirmVia` listener has; `Firewall`, its `ufw delete allow` undo commands. **A change that held nothing** (no `Drop`: `keyonly`, ufw's enable, a sudoers change, `pve-root`, and the migrations, whose listeners stay on their port) has no clean-up, and `hardn confirm` behaves as before: nothing is run after the claim but the timer's stop (`TestConfirmNothingHeld`; `TestRevertConfirmCommand` unchanged), unless the change confirmed before it left a clean-up that the claim's rename would lose (item 8, "carried"). `confirmed.json` is read before the claim for that.
2. **Who cleans up: the session that confirmed, over SSH.** `CheckConfirm` has just accepted it: a new SSH connection, made from another machine (not loopback, not the server's own address), to one of the listeners the change added, whose `sshd` process was not running when the apply finished. **A console** (the local-console exception, `ConfirmResult.Local`) confirms as before, with its warning, and **cleans nothing up** (`Held.Console`): a console shows nothing about SSH, so port 22 may be the one path that works from outside; the report says that it stays open and to run `hardn confirm` from a new SSH connection to the new port to drop it, and the exit status is 0, as before (item 10 says why). **A decision of this unit**, within P-40's "from the new connection": the clean-up's safety rests on that connection (item 4), which a console does not give (`TestConfirmConsoleCommand`, `TestReport`).

### The clean-up (`Cleanup`)

3. **Through the engine, under the run lock.** One control (`ssh.cleanup`) plans `steps.PlanCleanup` with the change's `Cleanup`; `engine.Plan`, then `engine.Apply`: the run lock, the plan made again under it and compared, the journal written ahead of each step (risk `other`), every change backed up first (`backup.RollbackRequired`), and on the first failure, Ctrl-C, `SIGTERM` or a hangup, the changes started rolled back in reverse from that journal in this process, with `SIGHUP` and `SIGINT` held (engine.md §2.4, §2.8 to §2.12; *since `next/s3-hangup`*, `SIGINT` ignored and `SIGHUP` caught, item 9). The changes, in brief 2.3's order: `ssh.cleanup/firewall` (`ufw delete allow 22/tcp`, only for a rule the change added that ufw still has, read back after), `/files` (`Drop port 22 from /etc/ssh/sshd_config.d/00-hardn.conf`: the Port lines without the held port, validated in context), `/reload` (`reload.Change.Confirmed`, which may only drop listeners), `/verify` (`sshd -T` must show the kept ports, on listeners, and the held one gone). Tested over the fake VM: `TestConfirmCleanup` (the commands after the claim, in order: Debian `systemctl reload ssh.service`; Ubuntu `systemctl daemon-reload`, `systemctl restart ssh.socket`; Alpine `rc-service sshd reload`; `ufw delete allow 22/tcp` first where the change added 22's rule; then `sshd -T` and what sshd binds show 2230 alone, the journal is gone, `confirmed.json` still holds the change, and a second `hardn confirm` finds nothing pending and nothing left).
4. **No revert timer of its own. Decision of this unit** (engine.md §7's option (b), which P-40 chose, reads "with no window of its own"):
   - **Why it is safe.** The confirming connection reached a listener the change added, on a port the clean-up keeps (CleanupOf leaves every `ConfirmVia` port out of `Drop`), from another machine. The clean-up only takes away: its reload is `Confirmed`, which `reload.PlanFor` refuses if it would add a listener ([reload.md](../safety/reload.md) §2.5), and its ufw step deletes rules for the dropped ports alone. Nothing it does can stop a new connection to the kept port, and the established confirming connection survives the reload: `systemctl reload ssh.service` and `systemctl restart ssh.socket` on Debian 13 and Ubuntu 26.04 ([vmtest.md](../safety/vmtest.md) §4.9, **run**); Alpine's `rc-service sshd reload` is **written, not run** ([reload.md](../safety/reload.md) §4). The check after the reload fails unless the kept port is still in `sshd -T`'s Port set and on one of its listeners, and a failure rolls back here. **In the planner** (`steps.Cleanup.Proven`, set by `Cleanup`): the changes take the class they have without the lockout risk (the reload `RiskServiceRestart`, the rest `RiskPlain`), so the engine journals the plan `other`, arms no timer and installs no boot unit, and `PlanCleanup` refuses unless the applying session is an SSH connection to a port the clean-up keeps (`ErrSessionNotKept`, `SSH_CLEANUP_SESSION_NOT_KEPT`), so that no caller can drop the port its own session came in on without a timer.
   - **What goes wrong with a timer of its own.** The clean-up would be a lockout-risk change waiting for its own confirmation, and `CheckConfirm` refuses the session that applied a change (`ErrSameSession`) and every connection open when its apply finished (`ErrOldConnection`): the user, just told that the change is confirmed, would have to open a third connection and run `hardn confirm` again within the window. One who does not (the likely case) gets the clean-up rolled back at the deadline, port 22 open again without a word but the next run's notice: the outcome P-40 removes. It would also replace `confirmed.json` with the clean-up's journal.
   - **What can go wrong without one** (the residual risk). hardn killed (`SIGKILL`, a power cut) inside the clean-up leaves `pending.json`, risk `other`, which no timer rolls back: the next run's notice names `hardn rollback <id>`, `Arm` refuses new changes until it is resolved, and the boot unit leaves it to that notice ([revert-boot.md](../safety/revert-boot.md) §2.7). The host is then either as before the clean-up (both ports) or with the held port dropped and the kept one listening; a `systemctl reload` or `restart ssh.socket` job, once systemd has it, finishes without its client **[unverified]**. A failed step whose in-process rollback does not finish is `ErrIncomplete`, naming `hardn rollback <id>`, as for any change (engine.md §2.13); a timer would have run the same rollback again later, the one thing it adds. Neither leaves the kept port closed by the clean-up's own changes.
5. **Every refusal `PlanCleanup` has is kept, shown, and changes nothing** (`TestConfirmCleanupRefused`): a `Port 22` line in `sshd_config`, which hardn does not edit (`ErrNoWriteStrategy`); a clean-up that would leave sshd no port (`reload.ErrDropsListener`, here with 2230 taken out of `00-hardn.conf` by hand); Proxmox VE's port 22 (`pve.ErrPortChange`, §3.18); a session not on a port it keeps (`ErrSessionNotKept`). With them, the engine's own plan-time refusals: the support gate, a change journalled, the backups; and, at apply time, before anything is changed, the run lock held by another apply and the plan made again under it differing (`ErrPlanChanged`). The confirmation stands in every case (`confirmed.json`), and the report says what stays open and how to finish (item 9). For the refusals that forbid dropping the port (Proxmox VE's port 22, sshd left no port, a session on the port dropped) it says why the port stays and gives no way to drop it by hand; after another hardn run (the lock, `ErrPlanChanged`, a change journalled) it says that whether the port is still open is not established, since that run may have dropped it (`TestConfirmConcurrent`, `TestReport`).
6. **Each step that fails is rolled back here** (`TestConfirmCleanupFails`, Debian with ufw, the change having added 22's rule): ufw's delete refused; the write of `00-hardn.conf` failing (a full disk); the reload refused; the check after it (`sshd -T` still showing 22); and the reload as each init system makes it: Ubuntu's `systemctl restart ssh.socket` and Alpine's `rc-service sshd reload` refused, the report's reload by hand as each starts sshd. Each time, under the signal hold: `00-hardn.conf` back with both ports, 22's rule back, sshd on both ports, the clean-up's journal gone, the confirmation standing; and the next `hardn confirm`, once the cause is gone, finishes the clean-up (item 8).
7. **Its backups go where the change's own went**: the parent of the change's journalled backup run (`<backupPath>/<id>`), else `backup.DefaultPath`. `hardn confirm` reads no configuration (the first-run prompt is `next/p3-startup`'s to fix), so this keeps `backupPath` without loading `hardn.yml` (`TestBackupRoot`).

### A crash between the claim and the clean-up

8. **The held port is recorded in `confirmed.json` alone, and the next `hardn confirm` finishes it** (`Held.Finishing`, `TestConfirmCrashAfterClaim`, `TestConfirmFinishCommand`). With nothing pending, `Claim` reads `confirmed.json` (`revert.ReadConfirmed`, new: root's alone, no symlink, decoded strictly, as `ReadState` reads the journal; `TestReadConfirmed`), and when `CleanupOf` of it drops a port that `00-hardn.conf` still has a `Port` line for (read from that file alone: `PlanCleanup` refuses a port configured anywhere else, so the file is what is left of the clean-up; all the ports when it cannot be read; `TestStillHeld`), and the session passes `CheckConfirm` over that journal, over SSH (it could have confirmed the change), the clean-up runs as in items 3 to 7. `hardn confirm` first says, as before, that no change is waiting for confirmation, with what the timer did to the change meant (a later change rolled back, or its rollback given up, is said first and its notice acknowledged as before, revert.md §2.22), and exits 1 whatever the clean-up does, since it confirmed nothing (130 when interrupted). Once the `Port` line is gone (the usual case, a stray `hardn confirm`), there is nothing to finish: it says only that nothing is waiting, exits 1, and runs nothing (files are read; `TestConfirmCleanup`). From a session that may not finish it (the one that applied the change, a connection from the host itself, an old one) or a console, it says that, that the port stays, and how to finish it, and runs nothing. **The SSH slice** can read the same: `steps.CleanupOf(*revert.ReadConfirmed(...))` (tested). The same path finishes a clean-up that failed and was rolled back (item 6), or was refused and put right by hand.
   - **Carried to the next change confirmed** (`Held.Carried`, `TestConfirmCarried`, `TestConfirmCarriedCommand`). The claim of the next lockout-risk change renames its journal over `confirmed.json`, so a clean-up left there (a console's confirmation, a crash, a clean-up refused or failed) would be lost to every later `hardn confirm`. `Claim` reads `confirmed.json` before the claim, and when the change it confirms held no port of its own (`keyonly`, ufw's enable, a sudoers change), and `00-hardn.conf` still has a port the change before it held, the clean-up is that change's: run from this session when it could have confirmed that change (a new SSH connection, from another machine, to a listener that change added, as for item 8), else not run, the report saying that the port stays, that `hardn confirm` no longer finds it, and how to drop it by hand from a new SSH connection to the kept port. A change that held ports itself (another port change) holds every port sshd was configured for at its apply, so the one left among them, and its clean-up deletes the ufw rules the change before it added for the ports it drops as well.
   - **Known limits.** A carried clean-up not run from that session (a console, a connection to the held port) is lost to `hardn confirm` once reported: the report says how to finish it by hand. `confirmed.json` keeps the change after its clean-up, and nothing records that the clean-up finished, so a `Port 22` line put back into `00-hardn.conf` by hand is dropped again by a later stray `hardn confirm`, or by the next confirmation of a change that held no port, from a qualifying session, which says so after the fact; hardn owns that file (brief §2). A record of the clean-up's end beside `confirmed.json` would close both, and is a change to revert's state directory (§7).

### The command line (`cmd/hardn`)

9. **The output** (`cmd/hardn/confirm.go`): the claim's lines as before (the legacy logger: `[SUCCESS] Change <id> confirmed (...): it will not be rolled back.`, the console's warning); then the clean-up's run through **pkg/output's renderer** (`output.Emit`, `FormatHuman`, of an `output.Run` in `ModeApply`: the steps, "Operation Successful", or the error report with each code, what was rolled back and the failing command's output); then the report of `Held.Report` as `pkg/ui` messages: what was cleaned up ("Port 22, which change <id> held during its confirm window, is dropped from …, and sshd, reloaded, no longer listens on it"; "ufw's rule for 22/tcp, which change <id> added, is deleted", or that ufw keeps a rule the change did not add, with the command that removes it), or what was not and how to finish it: `hardn confirm` again from a new SSH connection to the new port, or by hand (delete the `Port 22` line, `sshd -t`, the reload as sshd is started there, `ufw delete allow 22/tcp` for a rule hardn added), by hand alone for a carried clean-up, and nothing to drop for a refusal that keeps the port (item 5). The report states that a port is still open only where that is established (this run changed nothing, or rolled back, and the port's `Port` line was there: the change just confirmed wrote it, or `00-hardn.conf` was read to have it); otherwise it says that the state is not established and how to check it. The commands name the host's elevation tool when the clean-up's plan detected it (doas on Alpine); with no plan (a console, a session that may not finish it) they say `sudo`, as revert's own messages do, and add that doas takes its place where sudo is not installed. All of it on stdout, with **no colour off a terminal** (`stdoutEnv`, `pkg/ui/term`'s detection, beside `setRevertColour` for the legacy lines). The clean-up's run log (every command and its output) and the engine's and revert's lines during the clean-up go to `/var/log/hardn.log` alone (`revertFileLog`, `logging.LogFileOnly`), not the console, which the report sums up; the claim's lines go, as before, to both (`revertLog`). Ctrl-C, `SIGTERM` and a hangup during the clean-up cancel it (`signal.NotifyContext`, registered after the claim), and the engine rolls it back. *Since `next/s3-hangup`* (the maintainer, 2026-10-28: "Yes, catch it"; [engine.md](engine.md) §2.14): the hold around that rollback (`confirmHold`, `hold.SignalsAs("hardn confirm", …)`) catches a hangup as it catches `SIGTERM`, and says so as `SIGTERM`'s line does, a warning on the console and in the log, "hardn confirm: SIGHUP received; the rollback finishes first", where it ignored the hangup without a word; the commands the rollback runs are in sessions of their own. A hangup after the hold's release and before `confirmSignals`' stop, which ended `hardn confirm` by Go's default (the hold's `signal.Ignore` had ended that registration of it, and the release gave SIGHUP the default action), now reaches the registration, so `hardn confirm` reports and exits with the run's status (read). Its exit statuses are unchanged (item 10). `hardn confirm --help` says that it cleans up after a port change. Tested through the seams: `TestConfirmCleanupCommand`, `TestConfirmFinishCommand`, `TestConfirmCarriedCommand`, `TestConfirmConsoleCommand`; `newRevertSeams` points the clean-up's host at the fake and fails a test that runs the clean-up without stubbing it.
10. **The exit status** (`output.ExitCode` over the clean-up's run, [output.md](output.md) §2.8). **Decision of this unit: confirmed, but the clean-up failed or was refused, is 1.** The run did not do what it set out to do and the host is not as asked (port 22 open), and output.md's statuses give 1 to an apply that failed and was rolled back and to a refusal; 0 would tell a script that the host is hardened. The confirmation is reported first and stands, and running `hardn confirm` again is safe: it finishes the clean-up or says there is nothing to do. Interrupted: 130. Confirmed and cleaned up, or nothing held: 0. **Nothing confirmed: 1, as before** (revert.md §2.21), also when it finished a clean-up left by the last change confirmed (item 8): the command did not confirm, which is what a script asks it. **The exception: a clean-up not run from this session is 0** (a console, and a carried clean-up the session could not have run): nothing was attempted, the confirmation is what was asked and succeeded, and the report says what stays open; 1 would tell a script that the confirmation failed, which it did not. A console is a person at the host's console, who reads the report; a script confirms over SSH, from where the clean-up runs and its exit counts.

### Best-effort releases (P-41)

11. **Every plan made on a best-effort release carries one warning finding** (`engine.Plan.Findings`, new, additive), code `PLATFORM_BEST_EFFORT` (`engine.CodeBestEffort`), registered in p3-output's registry as a finding (`codes.golden` updated, and docs/reference/output.md §2.2), its detail naming the release and its matrix note: "Ubuntu 22.04 is a best-effort release: its vendor still supports it and hardn changes it, but hardn is not tested on it", "Alpine 3.21 is … (vendor support ends 2026-11-01)". Every plan, one with nothing to change included, as P-41 says. A supported release has none. **The gate is unchanged**: out-of-scope, unlisted and unestablished releases are refused for a plan with changes (`ErrUnsupported`) and get no such finding (`TestBestEffortFinding`, each support level, applied and planned again).
12. **Rendered with the plan**: the document's `findings[]` has it as the plan's own (no `control`, no `change`), and the human report shows it as a warning with its code below the plan, below an apply's steps, and below a flow (`AfterFlow`); it changes no exit status (`TestBestEffortRendered`). **A limit:** `pkg/ui/flow`'s `PlanView` has no place for findings (output.md §2.11), so in the interactive flow the warning shows after the apply, not before the confirmation; showing it above the plan is a change to the flow's screen, for the slice that wires the flow or the design system (§7). *Lifted by `next/s2-flow-review`* ([flow-review.md](flow-review.md)): the flow lists it above the plan, and `AfterFlow` no longer repeats it.

### Through `host.System`

13. Depguard rule `confirm-through-host`, the last of the rules, denies `os`, `io/ioutil`, `golang.org/x/sys`, `syscall`, `net` (but `net/netip`), `plugin`, `pkg/ui` and `pkg/output` to `pkg/engine/confirm`'s non-test files: it reaches the host only through `host.System` and the engine, and returns data, with the report's sentences (`Held.Report`), that `cmd/hardn` renders.

## 4. Written, not run (gate 5)

Nothing here has run on Linux or as root. On the VMs, [vmtest.md](../safety/vmtest.md) §4.11 (added by this unit) gives the steps; in short:

- **§4.4 step 9 now cleans up**: after `confirm 2230`, `sshd -T` shows port 2230 alone, `ss -Htln` shows 2230 alone, `00-hardn.conf` has `Port 2230` alone, the applying session on 22 survives the reload **[I]**, `key 22` is refused, `key 2230` works; on Debian 13 and Ubuntu 26.04 (`ssh.socket`: `daemon-reload`, `restart ssh.socket`) and Alpine 3.24 (`rc-service sshd reload`). ufw keeps 22's rule there (step 3's `ufw-enable` added it, not the port change).
- **`port --ufw enable` from an inactive ufw with no rules**, then `confirm 2230`: 22's rule deleted too (`ufw status` shows 2230 alone), the applying session on 22 surviving the deletion **[I]**.
- a refused clean-up (a `Port 22` added to `sshd_config` before the change: refused, exit 1, the confirmation standing); the console's confirmation leaving 22 open, then the next `hardn confirm` from a new connection to 2230 finishing it (exit 1: nothing was pending), the path a crash after the claim takes too (a `kill -9` between the two is hard to time on a VM); the console's confirmation, then `keyonly` confirmed from a new connection to 2230 carrying the clean-up (exit 0, 22 gone); the run log in `/var/log/hardn.log` and not on the console.
- on Alpine 3.24, the confirming session surviving `rc-service sshd reload` (reload.md §4).
- **P-41**: on a best-effort image (Alpine 3.22 or Ubuntu 22.04; none is among vmtest.md §5's yet), `hardn confirm`'s clean-up report, the one engine plan the command line makes so far, shows the warning; on Debian 13 and Ubuntu 26.04 none.

## 5. Changes to other units' code

- **`pkg/engine/steps`** (`next/p3-steps`'s; [steps.md](steps.md) §2.9 updated): `Cleanup.Proven`, `ErrSessionNotKept` (`SSH_CLEANUP_SESSION_NOT_KEPT`), and the write's summary, `Drop port 22 from …` instead of `Set Port in …`. Without `Proven`, `PlanCleanup` is as it was (lockout risk; `TestEngineCleanup` unchanged). Why in the planner: only it sees the ports left, so only it can refuse a session on a port it drops.
- **`pkg/engine`** (`next/p3-engine`'s): `Plan.Findings` and `CodeBestEffort` (`support.go`); `samePlan` does not compare findings (the plan made under the lock detects the release again, and its support is the gate's).
