# Confirm-or-revert (`pkg/host/revert`, `hardn confirm`, `hardn rollback <id>`)

> **Unit:** refactor plan Phase 2, `next/p2-revert` (brief 2.4; brief §2 decision "Confirm-or-revert: a 180-second default, confirmed from a new session"; decision §3.14, approved 2026-10-17: `--confirm-timeout` / `confirmTimeout`). V-numbers: the confirm-or-revert half of V6 ("no confirm-or-revert"), assigned to this unit, and the "no rollback anywhere" part of V18 ([side-effects.md](../refactor/phase0/side-effects.md)).
> **Status:** built and tested on macOS against the recording fake (`hostfake`), and on real files inside `t.TempDir()` for the journal; the signal hold of `hardn rollback` is tested on this process itself. Nothing has run on Linux: the timers, a rollback surviving the SSH session and hardn's exit, the `/proc` walk as root under sudo and doas, the console detection, and the brief's 2230 scenario are "written, not run (gate 5)" (§4).

A change that could lock the user out (SSH or firewall) is journalled in `/var/lib/hardn/pending.json` before its first step, and a timer is armed that runs `hardn rollback <id>` when the confirm window ends: `systemd-run` on systemd, a `setsid`-detached sleeper on OpenRC. Once the apply has finished, `sudo hardn confirm` from an SSH connection made after it, from another machine, keeps the change and stops the timer; otherwise the rollback restores the change's backups, runs its inverse steps, reloads the services it changed and leaves a notice that the next run of hardn shows. The Phase 3 engine writes the journal (`Arm`, then `Record` before each step, then `MarkApplied`); this unit defines it, arms and stops the timers, decides which session may confirm, and rolls back. One change is journalled at a time, so `confirm` needs no token.

## 1. Why

origin/main disables password authentication in the same step that adds keys, with no proof that a key works and nothing that undoes the change (V6), and has no rollback of any kind (V18). A confirmation typed into the session that applied the change proves nothing: existing sessions survive an sshd reload, and UFW normally keeps an established connection working while new ones are blocked (brief 2.4). Only a new connection, made after the change from another machine, shows that the user can still get in; when nobody makes one, the change must undo itself. Brief §3 principle 1: never lock the user out.

## 2. Guarantees

### The journal (`/var/lib/hardn`)

1. **What is recorded** (format: [`pkg/host/revert/testdata/pending.json`](../../pkg/host/revert/testdata/pending.json), compared byte for byte by `TestArmSystemd`; after an intended change, `go test ./pkg/host/revert -run TestArmSystemd -update` and review the diff): `schemaVersion` (1), `id`, `created`, `timeoutSeconds`, `risk` (`lockout` or `other`), a one-line `summary`, the `session` that applied the change (§2.10, with its connection's `sshd` process), `confirmVia` (the listeners a confirming connection must reach), the `timer` (kind, unit or pidfile, its argv, `deadline`); then, **written ahead of each step** (`Record`, §7), `backup` (the backup run's directory, `Manifest.Dir`, and the `seq` of each entry the change took, [atomic.md](atomic.md) §7), `undo` (inverse steps, in the order to run them: `run` an argv, `chmod`/`chown` a path back, `rmdir` a directory the change created, `plan` a unit's kept plan, §2.14) and `reload` (services to reload once the files are back: `ssh`, with `reload.Change`'s keywords, the listeners the restored files configure, `ifUp` while the forward reload may not have run, §2.14, and `socketDropIns`, the `ssh.socket` drop-ins the restore puts back that the change removed, added by `next/p2-revert-plans`, [revert-plans.md](revert-plans.md) §2.9); and last `applied` (`MarkApplied`: when the apply finished, and the `sshd` processes then running, §2.11). Arm sets `schemaVersion`, `created` and the timer; `Record` cannot change them, the timeout or the risk, and is refused once the change is applied (`ErrApplied`). A started rollback adds its progress and `runner` (§2.16).
2. **Checked before it is written and after it is read** (`Journal.Check`, `ErrJournal`): every field, since a rollback runs it as root. A command is a name (resolved from `host.SearchPath`) or a clean absolute path; paths are clean and absolute; `seq` values ascend from 1; a `plan` step names its unit (an id-like name) and carries valid JSON, and no other step carries either; a reload of a listener keyword (`Port`, `ListenAddress`, `AddressFamily`) carries `after`, and only such a reload carries `socketDropIns`, each a clean absolute `*.conf` path in an `ssh.socket.d` directory, once, in a journal with `backup`; a lockout-risk change has a timer, a window of at least `MinTimeout` and a session established by `ReadSession`; an `other` change has neither, nor `applied`. **Unknown JSON fields are refused** (unlike the backup manifest): a field this version does not know may be a step a rollback must run.
3. **Root's alone** (`ErrUnsafe`). `/var/lib/hardn` and every directory above it must be directories, not symlinks, owned by root and writable by neither group nor others; each file in it must be root's and not group- or world-writable, and is read with `ReadFileNoFollow` (a symlink there is refused). Files are written with `WriteFileAtomic`, `0600`; the directory is created `0700` (`TestStateUnsafe`, `TestStateRealFiles`). Otherwise a user who could write the journal would choose what root runs when the timer fires.
4. **One change at a time** (`*PendingError`, `ErrPending`). `Arm` refuses, before writing anything but the directory, while `pending.json` or `rollback.json` exists, and its message says how to go on: for a change still being applied, that it rolls back at the deadline unless it finishes and is confirmed, or `sudo hardn rollback <id>`; for one waiting, `sudo hardn confirm` from a new SSH connection (to the new port), or `sudo hardn rollback <id>`; for a rollback that is running, to wait (§2.16); for one that stopped, to finish it. **This holds for every change, not only lockout-risk ones** (a decision of this unit): there is one journal file, and a change that did not finish, of any class, is resolved before the next one starts. `other` changes (DNS, AppArmor, updates) get no timer (brief 2.4); they are journalled so that an apply hardn did not finish can be undone, and `Done` removes the journal once their apply succeeds.
5. **Claimed by rename.** `pending.json` becomes `rollback.json` when a rollback starts and `confirmed.json` when it is confirmed (that file keeps the last confirmed change). `rename(2)` succeeds for one of two processes renaming one file, so a confirmation and the timer's rollback at the same moment never both act: the loser finds nothing pending. After the rename the claimed file's id is checked; another change's journal put there since it was read is put back (`ErrClaim`, `TestClaim`). The loser's side is tested both ways: a confirmation that loses to the timer's rollback stops no timer (`TestConfirm`, "the timer's rollback claims it first"), and a rollback that loses does nothing (`TestRollbackClaimLost`). `Record` and `MarkApplied` read the state and then write `pending.json`; a claim between the two would leave `pending.json` written again beside `rollback.json`, so after the write they read `rollback.json`, and when it holds their change they remove the `pending.json` they wrote and return `ErrRollingBack`, before the engine's next step (`TestRecordClaimedMeanwhile`).

### The confirm window (§3.14)

6. **180 s by default, 60 s at the least** (`ResolveTimeout(flag, key *time.Duration)`): the flag when set (non-nil), else the key, else 180 s, rounded up to a whole second. **The floor is 60 s, and a value below it is refused with `ErrTimeout`, naming the flag or the key, rather than raised without a word**; an explicit `0` is such a value, never "the default" (`TestResolveTimeout`). Why 60: in the window the user opens a terminal, connects (to the new port), unlocks a key and types a sudo password (brief 2.4, which calls `iptables-apply`'s 10 s too short), and the window starts before the change is applied (§2.7), so the apply itself (validation, reload, the listener check, up to 5 s by `reload.DefaultAttempts`) comes out of it; under a minute the revert races a practised user, and a typo such as `18` for `180` would arm a revert nobody can beat. `ParseTimeout` reads whole seconds (`180`) or a Go duration (`3m`). **The flag and the key are not wired yet** (§7): there is no lockout-risk apply to time until `p3-engine`, and this unit changes no CLI surface besides the two subcommands.

### The timer

7. **Armed before the first step** (brief 2.4). `Arm` writes `pending.json`, then starts the timer, so a timer that fires at once finds the journal. When the timer does not start, `pending.json` is removed again and `Arm` returns the error: the change must not be applied (`TestArmTimerFails`). The removal, and the kill of a sleeper whose pidfile could not be written, run under a context of their own (30 s), so a Ctrl-C that stopped `systemd-run` does not leave a journal claiming a timer (`TestArmCancelled`). A `systemd-run` killed after it created the units leaves a timer whose rollback finds nothing pending. Which timer is `TimerFor`'s, a pure function of detect's `Init`:
   - **systemd** (`Init` Systemd, Running Yes): `systemd-run --unit=hardn-revert-<id> --description=… --on-active=<N>s --timer-property=AccuracySec=1s <hardn> rollback <id>`. The transient service runs "in a clean and detached execution environment, with the service manager as its parent process" (systemd-run(1)), not in the user's session, so it outlives hardn and the SSH session (§4). `AccuracySec=` "Defaults to 1min" (systemd.timer(5)), which would let the timer fire up to a minute late; it is set to 1 s.
   - **OpenRC** (`Init` OpenRC, Running Yes): `/bin/sh -c 'sleep "$1" && "$0" rollback "$2"' <hardn> <N> <id>`, started with the new `host.System.StartDetached` (§5): in a new session (`setsid(2)`: "Initially, the new session has no controlling terminal"), so the `SIGHUP` that the end of the SSH session sends to its terminal's foreground process group (POSIX `_exit`) does not reach it, and with stdin, stdout and stderr on the null device, so it holds none of hardn's pipes and cannot die of `SIGPIPE` once hardn exits ([system.md](system.md) §2.5). Its pid goes to `/run/hardn/revert-<id>.pid` (`0600`). The values are the shell's positional parameters, never part of the program text. **A shell may `exec` the last command of `-c`**: dash does (observed with dash on macOS: the command after `&&` took the shell's pid), and busybox ash's behaviour is **[unverified]**. So once the sleep is over the pidfile may name the running rollback; what keeps a cancel from killing it is the command-line check of §2.9.
   - **Anything else** (OpenRC installed but not running, an unknown init): `ErrNoTimer`, and nothing is written: a lockout-risk change is not applied with no way to undo it (`TestArmNoTimer`).
   - **Across a reboot**, which loses either timer: since `next/p3-revert-boot`, a boot unit hardn installs before the first `Arm` re-arms it at boot for what remains of the window, or rolls the change back once its deadline has passed, and the timer records the binary it runs (its path and SHA-256) and the boot it was started in, so that the boot unit acts only on a journal this hardn wrote ([revert-boot.md](revert-boot.md), [P-34](../refactor/proposals.md#p-34) (c)).
8. **hardn is named by its checked path** (`CheckExecutable`, `ErrExecutable`). hardn is outside `host.SearchPath` ([system.md](system.md) §2.2), so the timer runs `Options.Hardn`, which the caller takes from `os.Executable` and resolves with `filepath.EvalSymlinks`. It must be absolute and clean, a regular file with an execute bit, and it and every directory above it owned by root, not symlinks, and writable by neither group nor others: whoever can replace the file, or rename a directory on the way, chooses what root runs unattended when the timer fires. Debian's `/usr/local` is `0755 root:root` unless `/etc/staff-group-for-usr-local` exists, in which case it is `2775 root:staff` (Debian Policy §9.1.2); **on such a host hardn in `/usr/local/bin` is refused for the timer**, and the refusal names the directory.
9. **Stopped on confirmation and on a rollback before the deadline.** systemd: `systemctl stop hardn-revert-<id>.timer`. OpenRC: the pid in the pidfile is signalled with `kill -s TERM` only when `/proc/<pid>/cmdline` is exactly the sleeper's argv, so a pid reused since, and a sleeper whose shell has exec'd `hardn rollback <id>`, are left alone; then the pidfile is removed (`TestConfirm`, "the sleeper on OpenRC"). **A timer that cannot be stopped is harmless**: the rollback it runs finds nothing pending under its id and ends with nothing done (§2.13); the result says so (`CancelErr`: a pidfile with no pid, an unreadable command line, a failed `kill`, all tested).

### Confirming (`MarkApplied`, `Confirm`, `CheckConfirm`, `ReadSession`)

10. **Which session hardn runs in** (`ReadSession(ctx, sys, env, pid)`). sudo's `env_reset` keeps only "TERM, PATH, HOME, MAIL, SHELL, LOGNAME, USER and SUDO_*" (sudoers(5)), so under `sudo hardn` the SSH variables are gone; ReadSession walks hardn's ancestors in `/proc` up to init, reading each one's `stat` (comm, parent, controlling terminal, start time, proc_pid_stat(5)) and, until a connection is found, its `environ` ("the initial environment that was set when the currently executing program was started", proc_pid_environ(5); root passes its ptrace check):
    - **Remote**, with the connection, when `SSH_CONNECTION` is in hardn's environment or an ancestor's; Remote when `SSH_CLIENT` or `SSH_TTY` is; and either way the walk goes on to the **closest ancestor whose comm is `sshd` or `sshd-session`** (OpenSSH 9.8 split the server into "a listener binary, sshd(8), and a per-session binary sshd-session"): the server process of the session's connection, kept as `sshd` (pid and start time), which makes the session Remote too. A session with SSH variables but no such ancestor (a shell in tmux or screen, whose server init adopted) is Remote with no `sshd`, which cannot confirm (§2.11).
    - **Local only on evidence of a console**: an ancestor is `login` (a console login on Debian, Ubuntu and Proxmox VE, where Proxmox's web console runs `login` under `termproxy` **[unverified]**), or the last process below init has a controlling terminal that is not a pseudo-terminal: tty_nr's major is neither 0, nor 3 (BSD pty slaves) nor 136 to 143 (Unix98 pty slaves, devices.txt), as a virtual console (major 4, minors 1 to 63) or a serial line (major 4, minors 64 and up) is. That covers Alpine's console, where busybox `login` execs the shell **[unverified]**.
    - **Anything else is not established** (Remote, `ErrSessionUnknown`), so the local-console exception does not apply: `run0 hardn confirm` and `systemd-run --pty` (run0 runs the command "from a fresh, isolated service forked off by the service manager" with "an independent pseudo-tty", run0(1)), a shell in tmux or screen started at the console, a desktop terminal.
    When the walk cannot finish (no `/proc`, an unreadable ancestor, more than 64 of them), the session is what was found, Remote, and the error wraps `ErrSessionUnknown`: the local-console exception never applies to a session hardn could not place (`TestReadSession`). **This is a check against mistakes, not a security boundary**: whoever runs hardn as root can go around it.
11. **Who may confirm** (`CheckConfirm`, pure, `TestCheckConfirm`). First, **the apply must have finished**: `MarkApplied` sets `applied` after the engine's last step; until then the change is `ErrNotConfirmable` ("still being applied"), so a confirmation cannot claim the journal while the engine still writes it, and the notices say "being applied", not "waiting for confirmation". `MarkApplied` notes every `sshd` and `sshd-session` process running at that moment (pid and start time, `/proc/<pid>/stat` fields 1 and 22): the server end of every connection made before the change took effect. Then:
    - **The local console exception:** a session established as a console (§2.10; the zero `Session` is not one, `ErrSessionUnknown`) confirms, with a warning that this shows nothing about SSH (brief 2.4: losing SSH is not a lockout at the console). In this unit that is `hardn confirm` from that session; confirming in the applying session's own countdown is `p3-engine`'s flow (§7).
    - **Over SSH**, the connection and its `sshd` process must be known (`ErrSessionUnknown` otherwise), and it must be **new, from another machine**:
      - not from the host itself: a client address that is loopback or the server's own address is `ErrLocalConn`. ufw's shipped rules accept everything on loopback ("# allow all on loopback", `-A ufw-before-input -i lo -j ACCEPT`, ufw `conf/before.rules`), and no provider firewall sees such a connection, so `ssh -p 2230 localhost` typed into the applying session, a `-L 2230:localhost:2230` tunnel over the held port 22, or `ssh` to the host's own address proves nothing about the ruleset or reachability;
      - not the connection that applied the change (`ErrSameSession`; the same four `SSH_CONNECTION` fields), and **not one whose `sshd` process was running when the apply finished** (`ErrOldConnection`): an existing session survives an sshd reload and ufw keeps an established connection (`-m conntrack --ctstate RELATED,ESTABLISHED -j ACCEPT`, the same file), so the second session users keep open "just in case" shows nothing about a new one. A pid reused since has a later start time, so it is new. **A multiplexed session (ssh `ControlMaster`) shares its connection and its `sshd` process, so it is refused as the connection it rides on**;
      - with `confirmVia`, it must reach one of those listeners, the ones the change added (`reload.Plan.Adds`; `ErrWrongListener` otherwise): a connection over a held old port proves nothing about the new one, and the clean-up after the confirmation drops the port that worked ([reload.md](reload.md) §6). An unspecified listener address (`0.0.0.0`, `::`) matches either family, as a dual-stack socket takes IPv4 connections as mapped addresses.
    - **Known limits** (§4): a connection from another address of the host itself (a second interface) is not recognised as local; a connection accepted before the apply finished but authenticated after it has a post-authentication `sshd-session` that started after it, which the rule takes for new (the check uses the closest `sshd` ancestor); tmux or screen started at the console no longer reads as local (it is refused, which only costs a rollback).
    - **Since `next/p3-confirm-cleanup`** ([P-40](../refactor/proposals.md#p-40); [confirm-cleanup.md](../engine/confirm-cleanup.md) §2.2, §2.8): the session `CheckConfirm` accepts over SSH is the one `hardn confirm` cleans up from, with no revert timer of its own, since it reached a port the clean-up keeps; a console confirms and cleans nothing up. With nothing pending, `hardn confirm` applies `CheckConfirm` to the last confirmed change's journal (`confirmed.json`, `ReadConfirmed`), and finishes that change's clean-up only from a session it accepts, over SSH.
12. **Confirm** reads the journal, applies `CheckConfirm`, claims the journal (§2.5), stops the timer (§2.9) and returns the change. A rollback that has started is `ErrRollingBack` (too late); nothing pending is a `*NotPendingError`, which, when the timer undid the last change, says so: "was rolled back at …, because it was not confirmed within 180s"; a change that did not finish (`other`) is `ErrNotConfirmable`, naming `hardn rollback <id>`, and so is a lockout-risk change still being applied, with its deadline. A refused session's error says how to confirm: from a new SSH connection to port 2230, made from another machine, before the deadline.

### Rolling back (`Rollback`, `Abandon`)

13. **Idempotent.** `Rollback(ctx, sys, id, opts)` with nothing journalled under `id` returns `Nothing` and why (confirmed, rolled back already, another change pending, being rolled back by another process, §2.16, or its rollback given up, §8.1 item 7), with no error: the timer of a confirmed change ends there. An id that is not one (`../etc`) is `ErrID` (`TestRollbackNothing`).
14. **Claimed, then in order** (`TestRollbackByTheTimer`): the journal is claimed (§2.5), then
    1. the backups are restored: `backup.Load` of the run directory (which checks it is root's), the journal's entries only, `backup.Restore` (last taken first, idempotent, past a failed entry, [atomic.md](atomic.md) §2.16);
    2. the inverse steps run in the journal's order, **stopping at the first that fails**: ufw's rule, since an undo left out never locks anyone out and a later one run out of order may ([ufw.md](ufw.md) §2.14); a path that is gone is "already gone", not an error; `rmdir` removes only a directory; a `plan` step hands its JSON to the rollback `Options.Plans` registers for its unit, the unit's own checked rollback (§7), and a unit with none is `ErrNoPlanRollback`, refused already by `Record` and, in a hardn built without it, a failed step (`TestRollbackPlan`);
    3. the services are reloaded: an `ssh` reload is `reload.PlanFor` with `Change.Rollback` set, over detect's SSH facts read now and, for a listener change on a socket host, `ReadSocketDropIns`, then `reload.Execute` (`TestRollbackSocketHost`: daemon-reload, the socket's check, the restart of `ssh.socket`, the listeners). The drop-ins the journal names in `socketDropIns`, each of which must be the path of one of the journal's backup entries of a file (`backup.KindFile`, which the restore put back; `ErrJournal` before the reload otherwise, so that an edited entry cannot exempt another drop-in: `TestRollbackRestoresListenConf`), are passed as `reload.Change.RestoredDropIns`, so the rollback of a `listen.conf` migration plans its reload with `listen.conf` back in place, checked against the socket's listeners before the change, where before `next/p2-revert-plans` it stopped at `reload.ErrSocketDropIn` (`TestRollbackRestoresListenConf`, [revert-plans.md](revert-plans.md) §2.8). A reload journalled with `ifUp` (before the forward reload, §7) has `Step.Start` dropped: a unit that is up is reloaded, one that is down is left so, since the forward reload may not have run and the unit may have been down before the change; one journalled without it (the forward reload's gated step ran) starts a unit the change left down (`TestRollbackIfUp`);
    4. a timer that has not fired is stopped (a rollback by the timer itself leaves it, and removes the sleeper's pidfile).
15. **A backup that was not restored stops the reloads** (a decision of this unit; `TestRollbackRestoreFails`): the files on disk are then a mix of old and new that no check has passed, and a reload makes sshd act on them, which brief 2.2's "validate before activating" rules out. The inverse steps still run: the firewall's undo does not depend on sshd's files. The rollback is then incomplete (below), and the next `hardn rollback <id>` tries again. The trade-off is plain: while the restore fails, the applied configuration stays active in sshd.
16. **Resumable, and run once at a time.** Progress (`restored`, `undoDone`, `reloadDone`, reason, start) is written to `rollback.json` after each part, with the process running it (`runner`: its pid and start time from `/proc/self/stat`, and the boot's id from `/proc/sys/kernel/random/boot_id`), so a rollback that stops (an error, a crash, the deadline) is taken up by the next `hardn rollback <id>` after what it had done: a failed undo or reload is retried, one that succeeded is not run twice (`TestRollbackResumes`, `TestRollbackReloadFails`). **While the runner still runs** (same pid, same start time, same boot, not a zombie), another process does not take it up: the timer firing during the user's `hardn rollback`, or the user following the notice during the timer's, gets `Nothing`, "being rolled back by process N" (`TestRollbackRunning`); the notice and `Arm`'s refusal say it is running. Until it finishes, `ErrIncomplete` names the command to run, a new change is refused and `confirm` is too late. **A rollback that can never finish** (its backup run directory gone, an undo command removed) is given up with `Abandon(ctx, sys, id, opts)`, which renames `rollback.json` to `abandoned-<id>.json`, logs what was and was not done, and frees the journal; the host stays as the rollback left it (`TestAbandon`). `sudo hardn rollback --abandon <id>` reaches it since `next/p3-rollback-abandon` ([P-35](../refactor/proposals.md#p-35) (a), §8), and `ErrIncomplete` and the notice name that command; by hand, moving `/var/lib/hardn/rollback.json` aside does the same without the report, and stays the fallback (§8.1 item 6). The window between a claim and its first progress write is not covered: two processes claiming at that instant can both run.
17. **Not stopped by a cancelled context, nor, at the command line, by Ctrl-C or a hangup.** `Rollback` runs under `context.WithoutCancel` with its own deadline, `RollbackTimeout` (5 minutes): every `host.System` method refuses a done context ([system.md](system.md) §2.5), and a rollback half done is worse than one finished (`TestRollbackAfterCancel`). That covers contexts only: Go's default is that "A SIGHUP, SIGINT, or SIGTERM signal causes the program to exit" (os/signal). So `hardn rollback` ignores `SIGHUP` and `SIGINT` and catches `SIGTERM` (logging that the rollback finishes first) until it returns (`holdSignals`, `TestHoldSignals`). The ignored signals are inherited by the commands the rollback runs ("signals set to be ignored (SIG_IGN) by the calling process image shall be set to be ignored by the new process image", POSIX exec; Go's runtime, after fork, resets to the default only the handlers it installed, `clearSignalHandlers` in `runtime/signal_unix.go`), so the terminal's Ctrl-C and hangup, which reach the whole foreground process group, stop neither hardn nor the `ufw` or `systemctl` it runs. `SIGKILL`, and logind's `KillUserProcesses` once it escalates to it, cannot be held off: the timer, before the deadline, or the next `hardn rollback <id>` takes the rollback up (§2.16).
18. **Why it ran** is `Options.Reason` when set (the engine's `failed`, after a step failed with the session open), otherwise `timeout` once the deadline has passed and `requested` before it or for a change without a timer. A rollback by the timer leaves `reverted.json` (id, summary, when, the window, any failure) for the next run.
19. **Logged.** Every decision and step goes to `Options.Log` (`revert: rollback <id>: restore …: rewritten`, `undo 1 of 1: ufw delete allow 2230/tcp`, `reload ssh ([Port PasswordAuthentication])`, `done`), every command to the `host.System` run log. `cmd/hardn` sends both to the legacy log, `/var/log/hardn.log`, and the console: under the systemd timer the console is the journal, under the OpenRC sleeper the null device, so there the file is the record.

### The next-run notice (`Notices`, `Acknowledge`)

20. `Notices(state, now)` is pure and returns one plain-text paragraph per thing to say: a lockout-risk change still being applied (or whose apply stopped), with its deadline; one waiting for confirmation (until when, how to confirm, how to undo it now); one past its deadline that was not rolled back ("its timer did not run (a reboot stops it)", §6, [P-34](../refactor/proposals.md#p-34); since `next/p3-revert-boot` the boot unit re-arms or runs the timer at boot, so this notice is left for a change it does not act on, such as another hardn binary's, [revert-boot.md](revert-boot.md) §2.2); a change that did not finish; a rollback that is running; one that did not finish (with how to give it up, `sudo hardn rollback --abandon <id>`, §2.16, §8); and, from `reverted.json`, **"Change … was rolled back at …, because it was not confirmed within 180s."**, with any failure (brief 2.4). **`reverted.json` is only a notice**: one this version cannot read (another version's format, a hand edit, a wrong owner) is `State.OutcomeErr` and a notice saying so, never an error of `ReadState`, so it stops no rollback, confirmation or change (`TestBadOutcome`); it has its own version, `OutcomeVersion`, and is decoded leniently. `Acknowledge` removes `reverted.json` once a run has shown it.

### The command line (`cmd/hardn/revert.go`)

21. **`hardn confirm`** (no arguments) and **`hardn rollback <id>`** (exactly one) are new subcommands (brief §2 decision); `main.go` is unchanged: `revert.go`'s `init` adds them and the root command's `PersistentPreRun`, the notice. Both need root (exit 1 otherwise, with `sudo hardn …`), and both refuse `-n`, which only previews (exit 2, as `next/p0-hazards` does for V1). `confirm` exits 1 when it does not confirm; `rollback` exits 0 when it rolled back or had nothing to do, 1 when it did not finish, 2 for an id that is not one, and holds off signals while it runs (§2.17). `rollback` passes `revertPlans`, the registry of plan rollbacks: `plans.Registry()` since `next/p2-revert-plans`, empty before it (§7). Their output goes through the legacy `logging` package, as the rest of `cmd/hardn`'s does. Since `next/p3-rollback-abandon`, `rollback` also takes `--abandon`, which gives up an unfinished rollback instead of running it (§8). Since `next/p3-confirm-cleanup` ([P-40](../refactor/proposals.md#p-40)), `confirm` then cleans up after a change that held a port, through the engine, and reports it through `pkg/output`: it exits 1 also when the change is confirmed and the clean-up did not finish (refused, or failed and rolled back; the confirmation stands), and 130 when it was interrupted ([confirm-cleanup.md](../engine/confirm-cleanup.md) §2.9, §2.10).
22. **The notice** runs for every command run as root but `confirm` and `rollback`, which report the journal themselves; not as another user (the journal is root's, and nothing is read). **It goes to stderr**, so a subcommand's stdout stays its own: `sudo hardn system-details --json` during a confirm window prints JSON alone on stdout (`TestRevertNotices`). A "rolled back" notice is acknowledged only once a person has seen it: after a subcommand whose stderr is a terminal (a character device other than the null device); a stderr that is a pipe, a file or `/dev/null` keeps it for the next run. After the root command it is not acknowledged either, because the legacy main menu clears the screen and the notice would be lost: it shows again until a subcommand, `confirm` or `rollback` has shown it. `confirm` acknowledges it when it reports the change as rolled back already, `rollback <id>` when it has nothing to do for the change the timer undid. Neither does when the timer's rollback did not finish and was given up (`--abandon`, or by hand): each says so, and the notice stays (§8.1 item 7).

Depguard rule `revert-through-host` denies `os`, `io/ioutil`, `golang.org/x/sys`, `syscall`, `net` (except `net/netip`) and `plugin` to the package's non-test files (probed in a scratch copy: an `os`, a `net` and a `syscall` import each fail lint; `net/netip` passes); `host-stdlib` keeps it to the standard library and `pkg/host/...`. hardn's own path, the environment and the pid come from `cmd/hardn`.

## 7. Not in this unit; inputs to later units

- **`p3-engine`, applying a lockout-risk change:** `ResolveTimeout` over the flag and the key; `ReadSession(ctx, sys, os.Getenv, os.Getpid())`; `Arm` with `ID` the backup run's id (`backup.NewRunID`), the summary, the session, `ConfirmVia: revert.ListenersOf(reloadPlan.Adds)` (for a port change; [reload.md](reload.md) §6; for the `listen.conf` migration [sshd-migrate.md](sshd-migrate.md) §6), `Options.Hardn` from `os.Executable` and `filepath.EvalSymlinks`, and detect's `Init`. Then **`Record` write-ahead, before each step takes effect**, so that a rollback at any moment, or after hardn died or a `Record` failed, covers every step that may have run:
  1. a backup's `Seq` once the backup is taken and before the file it protects is written (a restore of a file that was never written is `Unchanged`, [atomic.md](atomic.md) §2.16);
  2. an inverse step before its step: ufw's `Plan.PreUndo()` before `Execute` and `Report.Undo` once it returns, as `OpRun` steps, last first ([ufw.md](ufw.md) §6); directory modes, owners and created directories as `OpChmod`, `OpChown`, `OpRemoveDir` before the chmod, chown or mkdir ([atomic.md](atomic.md) §7), each harmless if its step never ran;
  3. the `ssh` `Reload` with `IfUp` before the forward reload, and again without `IfUp` once the forward reload's gated step ran (`reload.Report.Steps[i].Ran`), also when `Execute` failed after it; a step that found the unit down (`NotUp`) leaves `IfUp`;
  4. after the last step, its reload and the listener check, **`MarkApplied`**, the apply's last write: until then `confirm` is refused, and a `MarkApplied` that fails (the `/proc` listing) is a failed step.
  A failed step: `Rollback` with `Options.Reason: ReasonFailed`. A `Record` or `MarkApplied` that returns `ErrRollingBack`: stop before the step, a rollback has the change; `ErrNothingPending`: the journal is gone (confirmed, or rolled back and gone), stop and apply nothing more; `ErrApplied`: a step after `MarkApplied` is a change of its own; `ErrNoPlanRollback`: a plan whose unit this hardn cannot roll back, do not run its step. While it waits, the original session shows the countdown and the command (brief 2.4; the countdown frame is a Phase 1 gap); on a local console it may confirm there (`Confirm` with its own session, after `MarkApplied`). Changes without lockout risk: `Arm` with `RiskOther`, `Done` once applied.
- **Stack B's units (`next/p2-sudoers`, `next/p2-dns`, `next/p2-alpine`, in `hardn-p2b`), for the merge before `next/p2-vmtest`:** each keeps a `Plan` "for p2-revert" and a checked `Rollback(ctx, sys, plan)` that undoes only its own writes. **Decision of this unit:** such a change is journalled as one `OpPlan` step (`Unit`, and the `Plan` as JSON), not as backups of its files, so the rollback is the unit's own (sudoers' `StageRestored`, DNS's resolved reload, Alpine's checked undo) and this package imports none of them; the step runs in undo order like any other. At the merge, `cmd/hardn`'s `revertPlans` registers, per unit, a `PlanRollback` that decodes the unit's `Plan` and calls its `Rollback` (under the rollback's context, already without cancellation), and the engine passes the same registry in `Options.Plans` to `Record` (which refuses an unregistered unit) and to `Rollback`. A `Plan` must therefore round-trip through `encoding/json` with every field its rollback needs, as each unit's doc already requires. The orchestrator may decide otherwise at the merge (journal those units as `RollbackRequired` backups plus `OpRun` steps, and drop their "kept for p2-revert"); the format here takes `plan` steps either way. **Registered by `next/p2-revert-plans`** ([revert-plans.md](revert-plans.md)): the merge (`next/p2-join`) left `revertPlans` empty, so `Record` refused an `OpPlan` step for every unit (`ErrNoPlanRollback`). `pkg/host/revert/plans` now registers the four, `pve-keys`, `sudoers`, `dns` and `alpine-updates`, each decoding its plan strictly (unknown fields refused) and running the unit's own `Check` before its rollback acts; its step functions make the `OpPlan` step, refusing a plan the rollback would refuse. `cmd/hardn`'s `revertPlans` is `plans.Registry()`, and the engine passes the same registry to `Record` and `Rollback`. `p2drive` journals its sudoers change ([vmtest.md](vmtest.md) §2.10). **`Record` does not check a plan's payload**: `PlanRollback` is one function, the rollback, so `Record` checks only that the step's unit is registered and its `plan` is valid JSON (§2.2); a step built by hand with a plan its unit refuses is journalled, and found only by the rollback, after the change (`ErrIncomplete` with `plans.ErrPlan`). The engine builds every `OpPlan` step with the unit's step function (`plans.PVEKeys`, `Sudoers`, `DNS`, `Alpine`), which checks the plan and its round trip first ([revert-plans.md](revert-plans.md) §2.4, §6).
- **`p3-engine`, a run lock:** `Arm`'s "one at a time" reads, then writes; two engines at once would both pass. The engine holds one lock per run. A rollback's claim between a `Record`'s read and its write is caught after the write (§2.5); only a rollback that also finished in that instant (microseconds) would leave a `pending.json` written again, then reported as overdue. **Since `next/p3-engine-boot`** ([engine-boot.md](../engine/engine-boot.md) §2.6 to §2.9): `Arm`'s check and its write of `pending.json` are under a journal lock (`JournalLockName`, `/var/lib/hardn/journal.lock`), which `Rollback`'s read, claim and first progress write, `Abandon` and `Boot`'s re-arm hold too, so no two of them interleave; `Record` and `MarkApplied` take none, and the limit above stays for them.
- **`p3-engine`, after the confirmation:** brief 2.3 step 5 (remove the old port's rule, drop the held `Port` lines; `reload.Change.Confirmed`) is a change of its own. `Confirm` runs no clean-up in this unit, so after a confirmation both ports stay open and allowed: safe, not yet hardened. The engine decides whether `hardn confirm` triggers the clean-up or the next run plans it. *Decided ([P-40](../refactor/proposals.md#p-40), 2026-10-19): `hardn confirm` does it, after the claim, from the connection that confirmed, with no revert timer of its own; built by `next/p3-confirm-cleanup` ([confirm-cleanup.md](../engine/confirm-cleanup.md)). `Confirm` itself is unchanged; `ReadConfirmed` is new, for a clean-up a crash left after the claim.*
- **The flag and the key (§3.14):** `--confirm-timeout` and `confirmTimeout` are wired with the first lockout-risk apply (`p3-engine` or the SSH slice), through `ParseTimeout` and `ResolveTimeout`; no `pkg/config` key is added here, so a legacy save writes no new field.
- **`p3-output`:** registry codes for the sentinels above; the exit codes (`confirm` 1 when refused, `rollback` 1 when incomplete, 2 for a bad id or `-n`); the words of the notices and messages, which are provisional; the log's path and the audit record of a rollback (today the legacy `/var/log/hardn.log` and the run log).
- **The main-menu slice (Phase 4):** show `Notices` inside the menu (a callout) and `Acknowledge` there; the legacy menu clears the screen after the notice today (§2.22).
- **The slices that move legacy SSH and firewall operations onto the engine:** until then the legacy paths ignore the journal: a legacy `-d` or `-w` during a confirm window is not refused, and a rollback would restore over what it wrote.
- **`next/p2-vmtest`:** the driver passes a hardn binary's path to `Options.Hardn` (not its own); the items in §4.
- **For the maintainer:** a reboot inside the window loses the timer (transient units and the sleeper are runtime state), so the change stays unconfirmed and the next run says it is overdue ([P-34](../refactor/proposals.md#p-34)); decided (c), re-arm at boot, and built in `next/p3-revert-boot` ([revert-boot.md](revert-boot.md)). A rollback that can never finish is given up with `Abandon`, which `sudo hardn rollback --abandon <id>` reaches ([P-35](../refactor/proposals.md#p-35) (a), decided 2026-10-19; `next/p3-rollback-abandon`, §8).
- **Size:** about 2,240 lines of non-test Go in `pkg/host/revert` (about 440 of them whole-line comments), 256 in `cmd/hardn/revert.go` and 145 in the seam, and about 2,670 of tests, against the plan's guideline of about 500: the journal and its checks (it is run as root), two timers, the session walk and its console rule, the confirmation rules (new connections, from another machine, after the apply), a resumable rollback over three earlier units that runs once at a time, the plan hand-off for stack B, the notices, the two subcommands with their signal hold, and a test per failure path.
- **`next/p3-engine`** ([engine.md](../engine/engine.md)): the engine writes the journal as this section says (engine.md §2.4, §2.10 to §2.12); the run lock is `host.System.Lock` on `/run/hardn/engine.lock`, a `flock` the kernel releases with its holder (§2.8); an in-process rollback that returns `ErrIncomplete` still stops the timer, a decision explained there (§2.13); a lockout-risk apply keeps the end of its window for the rollback in its own process, so that the timer's never races a change (§2.10); the clean-up after the confirmation is a question for the maintainer (§7). ## 8. Giving up a rollback (`hardn rollback --abandon <id>`)

> **Unit:** refactor plan Phase 3, `next/p3-rollback-abandon` (plan §2, Phase 3 row `p3-rollback-abandon`; [P-35](../refactor/proposals.md#p-35) (a), decided by the maintainer 2026-10-19). No V-number: the rollback that cannot finish is this page's own case (§2.16).
> **Status:** built and tested on macOS against the recording fake (`hostfake`), the command's parsing and wiring through `cmd/hardn`'s seams with no binary run. Nothing has run on Linux (§8.3).

A rollback that cannot ever finish (its backup run directory deleted, an undo command such as `ufw` removed) fails the same way on every `hardn rollback <id>`, and until it finishes every new change is refused (§2.4, §2.16). `Abandon` gives it up; this unit puts it on the command line, as the maintainer decided, and keeps the manual step as the fallback. Brief §3 principle 1: a rollback that blocks every later change, a lockout-risk fix included, must have a way out that says plainly what it leaves undone.

### 8.1 Guarantees

1. **A flag on the existing subcommand, and no other CLI change.** `--abandon` is a bool flag local to `rollback`, with no short form; `<id>` stays the one positional argument (`cobra.ExactArgs(1)`), so `hardn rollback --abandon <id>` and `hardn rollback <id> --abandon` are the same, and a missing id is cobra's usage error, as for `rollback` (`TestRevertAbandonCommand`). Without the flag, `rollback` is unchanged (`TestRevertRollbackCommand`, `TestRevertCommandsRefuse`). No other flag, subcommand, key or default changes (§3.11 is not approved).
2. **Root only, refused under `-n`, as `hardn rollback`** (`revertPreflight`, §2.21): `-n` exits 2 with the reason ("-n only previews, and hardn rollback --abandon changes the host: run it without -n"), a user other than root exits 1 ("must be run as root: sudo hardn rollback --abandon"), and in both cases the host is not reached: nothing is read or written (`TestRevertAbandonCommand`).
3. **Only an unfinished rollback of `<id>` whose process has gone, or cannot be named, is given up**; each refusal changes nothing (no command, write, removal or rename) and says what to do instead (`TestAbandonRefused`, `TestRevertAbandonCommand`):
   - while its process runs (§2.16's runner: same pid, start time and boot, not a zombie), `ErrRollingBack`, exit 1: "process N is rolling it back now (started …); wait for it to end, and if it does not finish, run `sudo hardn rollback <id>` once more before giving it up";
   - anything else, a `*NoRollbackError`, `Is(ErrNothingPending)`, exit 1. Its `Kind` says why (`Other` names the other change, `Kept` the record), and its text what to do: another change's rollback runs now (`other-running`: wait for that rollback to end); another change's rollback has not finished (`other-unfinished`: finish it with `sudo hardn rollback <other>`, or, if it cannot finish, give it up with `--abandon <other>`); the change is journalled and its rollback has not started (`not-started`: to undo it, `sudo hardn rollback <id>`); another change is journalled (`other-pending`); its rollback was given up already (`given-up`, with the record's path; or, for the timer's rollback, given up by hand, item 7); the timer rolled it back (`rolled-back`, when); it is not journalled (`not-journalled`). The message leaves out `ErrNothingPending`'s own words ("no change is waiting for confirmation"), which are about confirming and would be wrong beside a change that does wait;
   - an id that is not one, `ErrID`, exit 2, as `rollback`;
   - a journal it cannot read (`ErrJournal`, `ErrUnsafe`, from `ReadState`), exit 1, with a line naming item 6's fallback (`TestRevertAbandonFails`).

   **A process that cannot be named counts as gone.** A record with no progress (the rollback stopped between its claim and its first write) or with a runner of zero (the rollback could not read `/proc/self`, §2.16) names no process, so whether a `hardn rollback <id>` still runs it cannot be told. `Abandon` goes ahead, and the report says so and to check with `ps` (`AbandonResult.RunnerUnknown`; `TestAbandonResult`, `TestRevertAbandonNoProgress`).
4. **What it changes is §2.16's `Abandon`**: one `rename(2)` of `rollback.json` to `abandoned-<id>.json` in `/var/lib/hardn`, through §2.5's claim (another change's journal found there is put back, `ErrClaim`), and one log line, which now names the record's full path. It runs no command, restores nothing and reloads nothing (`TestAbandon` checks the rename is its only change), so it holds no signals (§2.17): the rename happened or it did not, and the journal is consistent either way. The record is the journal with the rollback's progress (`TestAbandon` decodes it). **Another hardn acting between its read and its rename** (`TestAbandonRaces`): when the record it took names a process that runs (a `hardn rollback <id>` took the rollback up and saved its progress in between), it renames the record back and returns `ErrRollingBack`; a rename lost to a second `--abandon`, or to a rollback that finished and removed `rollback.json`, is not a bare `ENOENT`: the state is read again and the refusal says what it holds now (item 3), or, with `rollback.json` of `<id>` still there, `ErrClaim` and the command to run again (`TestRevertAbandonFails`).
5. **What it says on success** (exit 0; `TestRevertAbandonCommand` checks every line): that the rollback of change `<id>` (its summary) is given up; that **the host stays as the rollback left it**, and hardn will not undo what the rollback did not; what the rollback undid and what it did not, from its record (`Unfinished`, pure: the backups restored, each inverse step in the words of the rollback's log, each reload, in the rollback's order, `TestUnfinished`); where the record is kept (`AbandonResult.Kept`, `/var/lib/hardn/abandoned-<id>.json`) and that `/var/log/hardn.log` has each step; and that new changes can be applied again. Progress is recorded after each part, so a part listed under "Not undone, or not finished" may have run in part: the inverse step that failed, or a restore, which goes on past an entry that fails ([atomic.md](atomic.md) §2.16) and is recorded only once every entry is back. With no progress recorded at all (the rollback stopped before its first write), every part is listed as not undone and a warning says that it may have done more (`TestRevertAbandonNoProgress`). **When the restore and an `ssh` reload are both left** (`AbandonResult.SSHUnchecked`, `TestAbandonResult`), a warning says that sshd's files on disk may be a mix of the change's and the ones before it, which no check has passed (the reason the rollback skipped the reloads, §2.15), that sshd reads them the next time it starts (a restart, a package upgrade, a reboot), and to check them now with `sudo sshd -t`, the restore's lines in `/var/log/hardn.log` saying which files came back: a configuration sshd refuses stops it at its next start ([sshd-validate.md](sshd-validate.md) §1), a lockout that the skipped reload avoided only until then. The record cannot say which entries came back, so the warning says "may". The words are provisional, like the rest (§7, `p3-output`).
6. **The notice and the error name the command; the manual step stays the fallback.** `Notices`' rollback-unfinished notice and `Rollback`'s `ErrIncomplete` say `sudo hardn rollback --abandon <id>` where they said to move `rollback.json` aside (`TestNotices`, `TestAbandon`, `TestRevertAbandonCommand`). Moving `/var/lib/hardn/rollback.json` aside by hand, as root, still frees the journal the same way, without the report: for a host where this hardn cannot run, or a `rollback.json` it refuses to read (`ErrJournal`, such as another version's format or a hand edit, or `ErrUnsafe` for the file itself), which `Abandon` refuses as `ReadState` does.
7. **After it**, `hardn rollback <id>` has nothing to do (exit 0) and says that its rollback did not finish and was given up, with the record's path, not that it happened; another `--abandon <id>` is refused with the record's path; and `Arm` takes a new change (`TestAbandon`, `TestRevertAbandonCommand`). A timer of the abandoned change that has not fired (a rollback that stopped before stopping it, §2.14 step 4) finds nothing pending under its id when it does (§2.13), and logs the same. **The timer's own rollback, given up** (`TestAbandonTimersRollback`, `TestRevertAbandonTimersRollback`): the `reverted.json` it left, which says that the rollback did not finish, stays for the next run's notice (§2.20). Neither `--abandon`, nor `hardn rollback <id>`, nor `hardn confirm` acknowledges it: `rollback <id>` says the rollback was given up (`RollbackResult.Reverted` is nil), and `confirm` that the change was not confirmed and its rollback did not finish and was given up (`NotPendingError.Abandoned`; `RolledBack` false). With `rollback.json` moved aside by hand (item 6) there is no record, and both say that the timer's rollback did not finish, with its errors, and was given up. Before this unit, that state was reachable by the manual step alone, and both said "rolled back at …" and acknowledged the notice.
8. **Known limits** (§2.16). `Abandon` reads, renames, then checks the record it took (item 4). It does not catch a `hardn rollback <id>` that takes the rollback up and has not saved when the rename happens: that one writes `rollback.json` again as it goes, beside the record, and the next run reports it as unfinished. Nor one that saves or finishes in the instant between the rename and the put-back: the put-back can replace its newer record with the older one, which its next write corrects, or bring back the record of one that finished, which the next `hardn rollback <id>` takes up again. Nor a runner that cannot be named (item 3). A second `--abandon <id>` after such a race replaces the first record with the later one: the same change with later progress, so only the earlier snapshot is lost, and the log keeps every step. A short lock taken by `hardn rollback <id>` and `--abandon` around their read and claim closes these (§8.5); until then, do not run the two at once.

### 8.5 Inputs to later units, and size

- **The run lock (the plan puts it in `p3-steps`; [README](README.md)'s `p3-engine` list):** `hardn rollback <id>` and `--abandon` should take a lock too, around their read and claim (`Rollback`'s read, claim or take-up and first progress write; `Abandon`'s read, rename and check), which closes §8.1 item 8. **It must not be the lock an apply holds for its whole run**: §7's "one lock per run" spans the confirm window, while the original session shows the countdown, and the timer's `hardn rollback <id>` fires inside that window. Waiting on that lock, or being refused by it, leaves the change unreverted when the confirmation never comes: the lockout confirm-or-revert exists to prevent. So a short journal lock of its own, never held across the confirm window, on which the timer's rollback never waits for an apply. **Built by `next/p3-engine-boot`** ([engine-boot.md](../engine/engine-boot.md) §2.6 to §2.14): the journal lock, `/var/lib/hardn/journal.lock`, around exactly those reads and claims, held across file operations alone; a rollback waits for it at most 10 s, then goes on without it and logs it; `Abandon` refuses instead. §8.1 item 8's races are closed in both orders (`TestJournalLockAbandonAndTakeUp`), and so is §2.16's window between a claim and its first progress write (`TestJournalLockTwoRollbacks`); the advice "do not run the two at once" no longer applies to this hardn.
- **`p3-output`:** registry codes for `Abandon`'s refusals: `ErrRollingBack`, each `NoRollbackKind` of `*NoRollbackError` (whose sentinel, `ErrNothingPending`, has words about confirming, so a code keyed on the sentinel alone would mislabel them), `ErrClaim`; and the exit codes of §8.4. `Unfinished` returns the parts as provisional prose lines; a `changes[]` entry per part (its kind, index, done) is for `p3-output` to shape with the JSON contract. The words of the report.
- **Phase 4's main menu:** the rollback-unfinished notice now names the command; a menu callout (§7) can offer it.
- **Size:** the unit as first built was about 580 changed lines (about 160 of non-test Go, 335 of tests, 65 of docs). The review fixes (the state after an abandon in `Rollback` and `Confirm`, the races of §8.1 item 4, the exported refusal and result, the two warnings, step 16) bring it to about 1,170 changed lines against `next/v0.4`: about 390 of non-test Go, 680 of tests and 110 of docs, over the plan's guideline of about 500. The tests are most of it: the refusal and race tables at both levels, each checking what changed, and the report checked line by line.
